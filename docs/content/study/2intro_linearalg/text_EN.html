Basic Information

  • Course Start Time: First Semester of Freshman Year
  • Exam Difficulty
    • Existence of Midterm Exam (the only midterm exam during college): Vividly remembered, due to the large amount of calculation involved
    • Learning Difficulty: 4/5, relatively difficult
    • Final Exam Difficulty: 4/5, relatively difficult
  • Textbook Evaluation
    • Linear Algebra and Its Applications
    • 5/5, highly recommended. As long as you read the book carefully, there should be no problems that cannot be solved.
  • Course Evaluation
    • Zeng Kun, a very good teacher. His lectures are very clear and he is quite enthusiastic. He emphasizes key points during the course (Please state the 12 equivalent conditions for a non-singular matrix!), so pay attention.
    • I agree with his approach to problem-solving: when encountering proofs in linear algebra that you can't understand, the best method is to 'give an example'. There are no too difficult proofs here, including the final exam.

Study Path

  • Class + Textbook + Exercises
  • The thread of learning linear algebra: It starts with solving systems of linear equations, which may be more abstract. You need to be sensitive to homogeneous systems, pivot columns, multiple solutions, one solution, and no solution issues. Gradually, you learn to understand it from a geometric perspective, understand the properties of its row space/column space, understand the relationship between rank and dimension, and even scaling and rotation transformations, which is the second layer; The third layer, you will understand it from the perspective of 'functions', ignoring the underlying details, and realizing that it is just a manifestation of linear transformations: through concepts such as eigenvalues and eigenvectors, condition numbers, determinants, etc., to characterize the properties of a function; In the end, when you write some programs in later courses, you find that matrices are the most convenient form for computers to organize data: we choose to study it because its existence is so natural and universal.
  • Nevertheless, a large part of this course is spent on introducing the intermediate basis theorem/similar diagonalization and other miscellaneous things, and later on, quadratic forms, positive definiteness, singular value decomposition are rushed through due to lack of time, and I didn't fully understand the principles at the time, and the differentiation of matrices was not mentioned much either, and later when I took numerical computation/machine learning/optimization, I had long forgotten everything, which was a bit painful.
  • If you feel that there are too few exercises and your hands are too rusty, I recommend watching the exercise classes of Bilibili UP Limeter. It's best to watch them before the exam, and the effect is quite good, for example, when calculating determinants, you might encounter the original questions.
  • If you have time, you can watch the teaching videos recorded by Professor Zheng Weishi, which should be on his personal homepage at the academy if I remember correctly.
