Many people (including myself) are not aware that 'living in the future' is a highly dangerous 'spiritual opium'.

One day, I was chatting with a friend and jokingly said that our time seems to have stagnated, while other friends are either getting married or having a second child, and some have even divorced, yet the two of us are just going through our days without achieving much.

Although it was just a joke, the issue of 'time stagnation' has always been on my mind.

I was thinking why within the same amount of time, some people are more productive, while others' time seems to have stagnated.

Later, I realized that my friend and I have a common point, which is that we are people who live in the future, focusing more on the future than the present.

This has created a problem, as we are prone to 'turn a blind eye' to what is happening in the present, placing all our hopes in the illusory 'future'.

I always felt that at some point in time, our lives could begin. But that moment will never come.

Because 'time does not exist'.

Many people have heard the concept that 'time does not exist', but only a few can truly understand its meaning.

Because it does exist, such as today, tomorrow, the day after tomorrow... now, past, future. Even birth, death, and everything in the world operates in a predetermined order of time, yet you say it does not exist?

How to deeply and truly experience the fact that 'time does not exist'.

It's simple: when you are in the present moment, recalling the past or looking forward to the future, you will find that only this moment is real, perceptible, and experiencable. The past and future do not exist.

They are just the retracing and extension of your consciousness; only the present moment is real and can be experienced by you.

The past and future can only exist in thought; only the present is the only reality.

When we realize that time does not exist and we only have 'this moment', what will we do?

We will apply all our strength and wisdom to this moment, instead of indulging in the stream of consciousness and letting time slip away in vain.

That's why within the same amount of time, some people are very productive, while others remain stagnant.

Because some people live in the present. When a person lives in the present, they solve what they see and do what they want to do, and in the continuous process of creation, life begins to 'produce'.

Those who live in the future either turn a blind eye to the issues of the present or place all their hopes in the future. They always feel that at some point, it will be their time to truly step onto the stage. In fact, that future may never come.

Because the future depends on the present, on what we are doing at this very moment. Because everything is happening right now.

So our life is not a continuity in time, but a continuity of creation. The more you create within the same amount of time, the more 'productive' your life becomes.

'Flow' is not time, but the various things passing through the static field you call space and the objects moving in this static field. Time is just a way for you to measure motion!

In other words, time is motionless (or even non-existent), and it is only after we 'create' 'actual' and 'material' things within a certain period that we can measure the meaning of time.

If within a certain period, we produce nothing, then the time within that period is 'stagnant'.

The biggest drawback of living for the future is that we always sacrifice the present and place all our hopes in the future. In fact, the future is now, and now is the future.

For example, I want to be a writer in the future. I always feel that I can only become a writer in the future. In reality, I can become a 'writer' right now.

Now I need to keep writing and continuously absorb knowledge in this field, treating myself as a writer. Only in this way can I become a writer in the future.

What we can't do now, how can we do it in the future? What we don't have now, how can we have it in the future?

Only by starting to do it now, do we truly possess it.

Many people say they are stuck in the past and live in the future because they subconsciously believe that time exists. If time doesn't exist, you at this moment are both your past and future self, always you.

For example, a patient with depression says he lives in the past. In fact, he doesn't live in the past, but he has always been that person from the past at this moment. Only time changes, and he himself has never changed. That's why it is said that in the world of patients with depression, time is stagnant.

It is precisely because of the concept of 'the past' that we ignore the present, thinking that as long as we change a certain point in the past, we can regain vitality. But we never think that our present self is the 'past' self, and as long as we change now, we can change the past.

Once you realize that 'time does not exist', you will find thatWe have no past, no future, only the present.

Once you realize this, you will find that the mental crises that plague most people nowadays, such as anxiety, internal friction, procrastination, and fear, are all due to the inability to live in the present.

Many people (including myself) have not realized that 'living for the future' is a highly harmful 'spiritual opium'.

A kind of mental addiction that can make us dependent and gradually damage our physical and mental health.

Why can't we live in the present? Because we are always looking forward to the future.

I don't take this job because there will be better ones in the future. I don't love him because there will be better people in the future. I don't start a business because I'm not ready yet.

In fact, the present is the best choice. Although there may be better choices in the future, they are based on the future you, not the present you. Most people gamble on the present for tomorrow, until that tomorrow never comes, and they realize that the best opportunity is always now.

For example, many people are unemployed now, and some always place their hopes in the future, always thinking that if they lie down a little longer, the situation will improve in the future.

And once we realize that we have neither a past nor a future, we will only use all our strength and wisdom at this moment, changing ourselves now, and thus changing the past and the future.

If he places his hopes in the future and does nothing, that future will never come.

When I understood this truth, I was no longer anxious, internally frictional, or procrastinating.

At this moment, whatever I want to do in my mind, I just do it. Because time does not exist, the past and the future are just retrospective and extension in consciousness.

I am changing the past by changing the present, and I am creating the future by creating the present.

Everything happens in the present and can only happen in the present.

I think this is the secret why life is so 'productive'.

Update. (December 18, 2024)

Recently, I've read many comments, and some people have truly grasped the meaning of 'living in the moment' that I mentioned. However, there are also those who have misunderstood it, equating 'living in the moment' with 'seeking immediate pleasure'.

The 'living in the moment' I refer to is focusing on 'this very moment', daring to face reality, standing on the ground of reality, and through 'self-creation' (solving problems) feeling the value of 'self' existence, thereby summoning the courage to do more things we want to do and making life 'productive'.

There is a kind of creativity in this. By 'creating the present', we 'create the future'.

Pursuing immediate pleasure seeks a state of 'instant gratification'. It does not create, it merely satisfies one's desires. If the 'present' is not 'creating', how can the future be possessed?

Although seeking immediate pleasure is 'living in the moment', it presupposes a premise: that we have no future. This 'moment' cannot lead to the future, so its essence is pessimistic, powerless, and an escape from reality.

One focuses on reality, the other escapes from it; there is a significant difference between the two.

I want to clarify one point: the future does not exist because it is not real; it is merely an extension of our consciousness, existing only in our minds.

So where is the real future?

It is in this very moment, in the present time.

The present is the future. How my future will be depends on what I do at this moment.

As a netizen in the comments section said: the future is today. Because you have already anticipated the arrival of the future before, today is it.

So have you ever thought about a question: today's you is the 'future' that the past you looked forward to. If you did not work hard in the past, you would not have what you have now. If you still do not work hard today, you will not have a future.

Everything can only happen in this moment.

So it's not that there is no 'future', but the future is now, and now is the future.

If you want to gain something in the future, you must take action now for the future to truly be realized. This is what I mean by 'living in the moment' - do not pin your hopes on the future, act now.

So when I make plans, am I daydreaming about the future?

No. Because now is the future, what I do now is what the future will become. From beginning to end, I only exist in this moment, until this moment becomes my future.

My confidence also comes from this: what I do now is what I possess. For example, if I persist in exercising for 2 hours every day, I will truly have a good physique in the future. I don't even need to daydream about the future; it materializes in my mind.

From a life perspective, placing hopes in the future reduces our 'life experience,' as we can naturally ignore the present. However, only by 'living in the moment' can we unleash our creativity. As we continue to act, life becomes richer, and life becomes 'productive.'
