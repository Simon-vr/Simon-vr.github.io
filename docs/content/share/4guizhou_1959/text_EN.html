Author Biography: Wang Minshan, born in 1925, participated in anti-Japanese grain requisitioning in Henan in 1938. He later moved south to Jiangxi and then west to Guizhou, where he continued to requisition grain for the troops along the way. After 1956, he served as Party Group Secretary, Deputy Director, and Director of the Guizhou Provincial Grain Department. Later, after the department was renamed the Guizhou Provincial Grain Bureau, he served as its Director before retiring from that post. He then joined the Provincial People's Congress, serving as a Standing Committee member and Deputy Director of the Rural Economy Committee. He retired in 1994.

Between 1959 and 1961, famine occurred widely across China, with Guizhou Province being one of the areas most severely affected. At that time, I served as the Party Group Secretary and Deputy Director of the Guizhou Provincial Grain Department. The following are excerpts from the diary I kept between 1959 and 1960 as a provincial-level grain administration official.

1959

January 1

At the start of the new year, the county party secretaries' meeting was in session. The central topic of discussion was the grain issue. Having experienced the Great Leap Forward and the "spiritual surge in production," some areas began suffering from grain shortages immediately after the autumn harvest of the previous year. Judging by the atmosphere of the meeting, the greatest concern was the fear of an even larger grain crisis.

January 2

At the prefectural party secretaries' meeting, based on central directives and the situation of the Great Leap Forward in grain production, the grain procurement task for the 1958–1959 fiscal year was set at 3.6 billion jin (approx. 180,000 tons). Five months had passed since the autumn harvest, yet only 2.5 billion jin had been stored, of which 500 million jin were falsified figures. There was no further progress in storage.

January 5

The central government arranged for Guizhou to transfer out 200 million jin of grain. After consulting repeatedly with Secretary Zhou Lin and Vice Governor Xu Jiansheng, their unanimous opinion was to refuse acceptance, instructing the Grain Department to report this to the Ministry of Grain. The provincial department naturally supported Zhou and Xu's opinion. We had appealed many times, but the Ministry of Grain paid no heed.

January 7

Vice Governor Xu Jiansheng spoke at the county party secretaries' conference, making a profound self-criticism regarding the deaths caused by grain shortages in Xingyi in 1957. He stated that the central government had circulated a bulletin on the "Xingyi Incident" at the Wuchang Conference. (Note: In 1957, Xingyi County engaged in fraud, resulting in hundreds of deaths due to grain shortages; the county party secretary was subsequently disciplined.)

January 15

In the evening, the Central Committee held a telephone conference. Three central leaders—Peng Zhen, Tan Zhenlin, and Li Xiannian—all spoke on the grain issue. The focus of the meeting was emphasizing the stabilization of the rural grain situation and preventing deaths after a bumper harvest. Li Xiannian stated that the Great Leap Forward of 1958 was beyond doubt and its achievements must be affirmed; the "dark side" should not be publicized, and we should handle it seriously. It would be bad for the masses to endure a year of the Great Leap Forward only to be scolded at the end.

Since the Great Leap Forward meant a doubling of grain production, which was beyond doubt, why was there a grain shortage, and why did we need to prevent starvation? This extremely contradictory phenomenon was difficult to understand.

January 17

The autumn oilseed procurement task was 260 million jin, but only 14% had been completed so far. The Ministry of Finance and Trade held a special telephone conference on this matter. The key issue was that the Great Leap Forward had made labor extremely scarce; engaging in "large corps battles" regardless of consequences meant that no one attended to the collection and management of oilseed crops.

February 2

The meeting of grain bureau directors from various prefectures lasted three days. Statistics from the meeting showed that in 47 counties, farmers' retained rations were less than 200 jin of raw grain per person. It was shocking that farmers retained so little grain after a supposed bumper harvest. I found the estimated production figure of 18 billion jin for the previous year questionable. Losses and waste after the autumn harvest were astonishing, closely linked to the policy of "eating freely with bellies full." Many materials indicated that in the four months following the autumn harvest, each person consumed 250 jin of grain; one-third of the time accounted for half of the rations. The saying circulating nationwide was, "Eat tightly for the first four months, eat scantily for the next eight." We were no exception.

February 6

In the afternoon, I listened to Secretary Zhou Lin convey the spirit of the central "First Secretaries of Provinces Meeting." The theme was "a national chessboard," stirring up enthusiasm and creating high tides. Chairman Mao said, "I do not wish to stay in Beijing; there is no fresh air here. Since the Wuchang Conference, a wave of 'cold air' has emerged. We must correctly estimate the Great Leap Forward of 1958, affirm achievements, and treat shortcomings seriously." I wondered if the different voices heard regarding the Great Leap Forward were being labeled as "cold air."

Zhou also mentioned that the central government assigned Guizhou a task to transfer 200 million jin of grain in the first half of 1959. Initially, he haggled, but seeing the situation turn unfavorable, he accepted the task readily. When the central leadership asked about difficulties, he cited transportation issues. The central leaders replied, "Why did we build railways for you?"

February 8 (Lunar New Year's Day)

At today's prefectural party secretaries' meeting, addressing the clamor that grain yield reports were decreasing, Zhou Lin stated that the 1958 grain output of 18 billion jin could not slide down any further. In my view, this remained a mystery; we would be grateful if it were 12 billion jin. With such high estimates, no matter how we calculated, we could not find where the grain had gone. (Note: The final verified provincial grain output for 1958 was 10.4 billion jin, which was not an increase from 1957, but actually a decrease of 300 million jin.)

February 17 (Luodian)

Accompanied by County Party Secretary Feng and Director Wang, I visited several communes in Moyang, Kedu, Tongzhou, and the county seat. We found that many canteens had run out of grain, with some serving only thin gruel. In the county seat commune, seven horses died. Rumors among the populace spoke of the emergence of the "Jade Emperor," claiming he would raid grain warehouses, causing widespread panic.

This area is a producer of tung seeds. All labor had been diverted to the Great Steel Campaign, leaving tung seeds to rot on the mountains unharvested, resulting in pure loss. Comparing gains and losses, which was greater? This deserves deep reflection.

I discussed with the county committee the urgent planting of early-maturing crops to survive the summer shortage. I suggested they plant as many melons and beans as possible. The county committee immediately issued an urgent notice to all communes.

February 18 (Dushan)

From the reports by County Party Secretaries Cheng and Liu, the situation was poor. Grain procurement progress was only 58% complete, but there had been multiple incidents of rural grain shortages. Some canteens had stopped serving meals, and members refused to work.

From what I saw in several counties, the provincial decision to provide each person with 14 liang (i.e., 0.875 jin) of raw grain had largely been implemented. However, the tense atmosphere regarding grain remained heavy. I even heard considerable dissatisfaction with the government and the Great Leap Forward, with some cursing the Great Leap Forward and the supposed bumper harvest.

February 25 (Duyun)

The prefectural committee was holding a massive oath-taking rally for the Great Leap Forward, aiming to launch a new high tide in industrial and agricultural production. Challenge letters, guarantee letters, and determination letters were held high in the square. The slogan was to achieve an even greater leap than in 1958. The grain production plan for this year was increased at every level. The province assigned Qiannan a plan of 3.9 billion jin; the prefecture increased this to 4.2 billion jin when distributing to counties; and counties raised it further to 6.2 billion jin when assigning to communes. It was said that Secretary Jinfeng was under great pressure, as Qiannan had ranked in the lower-middle tier of the province during last year's Great Leap Forward.

I reported in detail to Secretary Jinfeng on the situations I observed in several counties. He said he had addressed the problems I saw in his report to the four-level cadre meeting. He also stated that the prefecture's grain procurement task would definitely not be completed; achieving 80% would be considered good.

At the meeting of grain station masters in Duyun City, the feedback was that rural grain difficulties this year would exceed any previous year, with widespread panic.

The trip to Qiannan added greatly to my anxiety. Currently, about one-quarter of the area faced grain shortages, and the trend of spread and expansion was rapid.

February 28 (Majiang)

I met with County Party Secretaries Li and Tian, who consistently reported that the pressure of the grain procurement task was too great and impossible to fulfill. Simultaneously, rural areas lacked grain and required resales; at least 5 million jin were needed to get by. I expressed complete sympathy and understanding for the data and specific materials they presented. The root cause lay in the actual grain yield.

March 1 (Kaili)

The prefecture introduced that Kaili was a large county formed by merging four counties (Leishan, Majiang, Lushan, and Danzhai). The problems reported included significant outward migration due to grain issues, and it was rumored that "bad elements" were causing sabotage.

At the prefectural committee, I saw a central document forwarding Zhao Ziyang's investigation in Guangdong on rural concealment and private division of grain. The amount concealed and privately divided accounted for 50% of total production. All provinces were holding meetings and launching campaigns against concealment and private division. Pinning hopes for the success of the Great Leap Forward in grain production on cracking down on concealment and private division was not suitable for local conditions everywhere.

March 2 (Guiding)

County Party Secretaries Yu and Feng introduced that grain was already very tight in Yanshan and Pingfa districts. Some places had run out of food and stopped cooking, while others ate only thin gruel, seriously affecting members' willingness to work. Secretary Yu and I visited some households in communes and teams in Yanshan. The situation was indeed as described. I suggested to the county committee that measures to resolve the issue be arranged early.

March 7

For several days, Vice Governor Xu was anxious as fire, criticizing the poor completion of the grain transfer task. We were overwhelmed trying to complete the central task of transferring grain to Shanghai. Despite contacting the Department of Transportation multiple times, we could not arrange vehicle capacity or labor, especially failing to solve the problem of labor for collecting and transporting grain from rural areas.

March 9

Zhou Lin conveyed the spirit of the Central Zhengzhou Meeting. Chairman Mao said that the relationship between local party committees and grassroots cadres of people's communes was currently in a very tense state. The rural uproar over grain was worse than in 1954. The essence of this contradiction stemmed from ownership issues and the policies we adopted. This tense situation was caused by "egalitarianism, leveling the rich and poor," "gratuitous conscription of labor and materials," and "excessive recovery of historical agricultural loans." Simply put, the effects of "leveling," "conscription," and "collection" were destructive.

Mao also said that after the autumn harvest last year, people's communes adopted measures of "deep hiding and distant窖ing (cellaring), standing guard and posting sentries" to protect their products. Could this be viewed as a self-defensive behavior by farmers against "equalization and gratuitous transfer" and to cope with high procurement quotas?

March 15

In recent days, I have heard many reports: either this place lacks grain and has stopped cooking, or that place has stopped fires and people are fleeing. This makes me uneasy. What is the truth? I am not sure. However, I feel that our excessive propaganda of a bumper harvest and the policy of not supplying grain in rural areas are far removed from reality.

March 21

Hu Chongshan and several other department and bureau directors returned from inspecting work in Zunyi. They reported that the problems there were quite serious. Grain procurement was difficult to complete, and recently, waves of rural grain shortages had erupted, particularly severe in Zunyi, Meitan, and Tongzi. Incidents of illness and death from starvation were occurring continuously.

March 24

The grain procurement task was 3.2 billion jin, but the storage progress was still short by 600 million jin. There was no further progress. Vice Governor Xu also felt that the possibility of further storage was slim, reflecting problems with the reported grain yields.

March 27

I discussed the transportation of the 200 million jin grain transfer with the Provincial Department of Transportation. Their attitude was that transport capacity was extremely tight. Steel was the "marshal" and had to be prioritized; timber and disaster relief grain also had to be guaranteed, but vehicle capacity was truly difficult to arrange.

March 29

Many provinces reported news of widespread rural concealment and private division of grain. However, I had not seen such typical cases in our province, nor were such materials visible at the five-level cadre meetings in various regions. Secretary Zhou Lin also stated at the provincial six-level cadre meeting that our province had serious situations of concealment and private division. There were rumors everywhere that "grain was eaten by big-beaked crows" and stories of "catching ghosts to seize grain." What was the truth? I believed the situation should not be overestimated. Relying on cracking down on concealment to survive the shortage would fail.

March 31

Rural areas were severely short of grain, with emergency telegrams arriving constantly from all locations. In the evening, the provincial party committee held a telephone conference with prefectural and county secretaries. Vice Governor Xu made a decisive and clear policy announcement: "Wherever grain shortages are discovered, grain must be supplied first, and then the situation investigated. Do not delay and cause deaths while investigating." Opening this channel was of decisive significance in reducing incidents. It gave us the initiative in work, but the originally planned 300 million jin of resale grain would definitely be exceeded.

April 3

The National Grain Directors' Conference had been going on for three days. From the reports of various provinces, it was a chorus of cries. Grain purchase, sales, transfer, and storage were all in a state of tension unprecedented since the founding of the People's Republic. Instability had appeared in the rural areas of many provinces; the situation was no better than in Guizhou. In Hubei, tens of thousands of people were fleeing famine, and canteens could no longer operate; distribution to households was becoming widespread.

During the speeches, many provinces expressed frustration with close planting. Sowing 50-60 jin of seed per mu of wheat was shockingly wasteful and completely unscientific blind command. Minister Yu Jie commented that it was "petty-bourgeois fanaticism." When I reported on compressing sales volume, mentioning the compression of grain used for brewing, Minister Yu immediately interjected, "The grain for Maotai liquor cannot be compressed!"

April 10 (Beijing)

Minister Chen Guodong conveyed the spirit of the Second Plenary Session of the Seventh Central Committee held in Shanghai. Chairman Mao said, "Grain is tight; who told you to brag excessively? For ten years, do not talk about completely solving the grain problem." Li Xiannian said, "We must pass through the critical months of April, May, June, July, and August. Work hard with heads down, and prepare to suffer a serious illness to get through the difficulty." Minister Chen Guodong said there were two painful lessons from grain work in 1958: First, in grain procurement, "he who acts first gains strength, he who acts last suffers disaster"; we were too optimistic. Second, do not speak too absolutely; the bigger the brag, the greater the difficulty.

April 14 (Beijing)

Just before adjourning, the minister had a private conversation with me, urging Guizhou to strive to exceed the planned grain transfer task in the second quarter. He also said that two nights ago, Chairman Mao told Vice Premier Li Xiannian, "Grain transfer and transportation must be handled like warfare; progress must be mastered daily. The third quarter must overcome the difficulty." I told Minister Zhao that we were experiencing grain shortages everywhere, deaths had already occurred, the transfer task was hard to guarantee, exceeding it was hopeless, and Guizhou was having a hard time.

April 23

The Central Committee sent an urgent telegram to the Provincial Party Committee and the Grain Department Party Group, requiring that "Party committees at all levels must currently regard grain work as one of the most important tasks. Implement more centralized leadership principles in grain work, implement unified scheduling. Rural grain supply can only be provided in heavily disaster-stricken areas and economic crop zones. In cities, apart from guaranteeing the essential supply for heavy manual laborers, all compressible sales volumes must be resolutely compressed. Transfer orders for grain tasks assigned by provinces to the center must be resolutely executed and guaranteed to be completed..." The focus of the telegram was extremely clear: First, highly unified and centralized grain control; Second, compress local sales volume to ensure transfers to the center.

It was rare for the Central Committee to send a telegram directly to a provincial department/bureau party group.

April 26

I saw the Central Committee's "Instruction on Earnestly Compressing Grain Sales Volume" issued on April 21. It required urban sales volume to be compressed by about one-tenth from the current base, and rural grain sales volume to be reasonably compressed. To concentrate forces to deal with the difficult situation during this period, all localities must resolutely complete the grain transfer orders issued by the center.

April 30

The Ministries of Transportation, Grain, and Railways jointly held a telephone conference, emphasizing the arrangement of transport capacity to guarantee grain transfers to the center. This task was to be raised to an important agenda item for party committees. The Minister of Railways said that transport arrangements prioritizing steel and coal must all yield to grain transportation, making every effort to guarantee the completion of transfer and export tasks.

May 1

May Day Labor Festival, universal celebration. A rally of 500,000 people in Beijing, and over 60,000 in Guiyang, vigorously summarized the Great Leap Forward of 1958 and stirred up enthusiasm to strive for an even greater leap in 1959. In the evening, the streets were decorated with lanterns and banners, brilliantly lit. However, the shelves for pastries, fruits, and all edible non-staple foods in stores were empty. It could be described as "strong on the outside, dry on the inside."

May 3

Vice Governor Xu called from Beijing, saying the central government had assigned our province a grain transfer task of 150 million jin for the third quarter, to be completed in full. I harbored a侥幸心理 (wishful thinking), hoping to drag it out if possible, but the center did not let go.

May 17

At the regional grain directors' meeting, we cleared the major accounts of grain inventories in various regions. The aggregated figures reported by the regions were shocking and worrying.

Current grain inventory decreased by 200 million jin;

Summer grain procurement fell short by 200 million jin;

Grain sales demand increased by 200 million jin;

Grain transferred to the province was requested to be reduced by 200 million jin.

Calculating this, by the autumn harvest at the end of September, only 200 million jin of inventory would remain.

Crisis! Crisis!

May 25

The Provincial Party Standing Committee met last night. Vice Governor Xu conveyed the spirit of Vice Premier Li Xiannian's telephone conference, stating that the current tension in edible oil, except for not causing deaths, was no less than that of grain. Extraordinary measures must be taken to compress sales. After research, it was decided to compress urban supply by half, cease rural supply of edible oil, and guarantee the completion of export tasks.

June 3

Zhou Lin and Xu Jiansheng made a great determination to export edible oil, ordering us to make arrangements overnight. I was designated to personally call the first secretaries of various prefectural committees to convey the provincial committee's intention, requiring emergency measures to guarantee completion.

June 5

This year's summer grain output was set at 800 million jin, with a procurement target of 400 million jin. Vice Governor Xu expressed full confidence. Judging by the recent slow progress of storage, I expressed some concern about the yield and felt the procurement quota was too high. Xu criticized me for being right-leaning and conservative.

June 18 (Jinsha County)

From the situation described by County Magistrate Qiu Jigang, Jinsha County was a nationally famous "Red Flag County" of the Great Leap Forward, yet the problems in rural areas were severe. This was inseparable from the severity of the exaggeration wind. (Note: According to the Jinsha County Gazetteer, the total population of the county was 330,000 in 1959 and dropped to 249,000 in 1960, a decrease of over 80,000 people.)

Magistrate Qiu introduced the experience of making wo-wo-tou (steamed cornbread) from rapeseed cake after oil extraction. I was very interested and immediately sent this information to Vice Governor Xu and Minister Li Tinggui, suggesting vigorous promotion.

June 20 (Zunyi)

I discovered that in many places in Zunyi, in order to complete the summer grain procurement task, even the seeds retained by farmers were procured. This practice, which harmed the people and wasted resources, persisted despite prohibitions.

July 9 (Dafang)

I reported the situations of Changshi and Piao'erjing communes to County Party Secretary Yang Benhe. I stated that the practice of confiscating retained seeds during summer grain procurement must be resolutely stopped. Yang said that 400,000 jin of seeds had already been stored in the county warehouse, counting towards the procurement task.

July 11 (Puding)

Upon meeting me, County Party Secretary Zhao Fengguang strongly reported that the summer grain procurement task of 5 million jin required mobilizing the entire party and going all out, yet only 3 million jin had been completed. The prefectural committee had named and shamed them multiple times, and now there was truly no grain left.

August 4

Secretary Miao conveyed the telephone record from Zhou Lin at the Central Lushan Conference, calling for opposing right-leaning tendencies, opposing slackness, and stirring up enthusiasm to fight fiercely in July, August, and September. Thus, hope for the 18 billion jin grain yield was revived again, fifty or sixty billion more than our estimate.

August 12

The Provincial Financial and Trade Work Conference (attended by financial and trade ministers, grain directors, and finance directors from various regions) conveyed central instructions. Chen Yun said, "The grain problem depends on politics for eating, and must be solved through politics." Li Xiannian said, "Grain is politics."

In the concluding speech of the conference, Vice Governor Xu reaffirmed that the procurement task of 3 billion jin must be completed.

During the group discussions in the Zunyi and Qiannan groups that I attended, everyone said that many counties had conveyed the spirit of Vice Premier Li Xiannian's phrase "He who acts first gains strength, he who acts last suffers disaster" to the grassroots. The determination expressed in the discussions was to complete the procurement task even if it meant retaining no seeds. This caused great chaos and would surely have future repercussions.

August 26

Significant news arrived: The Eighth Plenary Session of the Eighth Central Committee was held at Lushan, criticizing the Peng Dehuai Right-Opportunist Anti-Party Clique. They were accused of attacking the Great Leap Forward, opposing people's communes, denying the great achievements of the Great Leap Forward, and exaggerating shortcomings, etc.

September 13 (Renhuai)

Problems in Renhuai during procurement and storage were also serious. Rushing to store grain urgently, communes and teams that suffered disasters and reduced yields were still required to store grain. I visited two production teams, both planting cotton, yet they were assigned grain procurement tasks. The county committee introduction also mentioned that 50,000 farmers in the county planted cotton but were assigned a 1 million jin grain procurement task. Situations arose where people carried grain to pay procurement taxes in the morning and bought grain from the warehouse to eat in the afternoon.

Although I repeatedly propagated the policy of not procuring excessive grain wherever I went, the effect seemed limited. One must recognize the objective factor of excessive pressure from procurement tasks. The problem still lay at the top.

September 15 (Zunyi)

In the morning, I called the Sichuan Grain Department. Their efforts to compress sales were significant. Residents' monthly grain ration in Chengdu was compressed from 24 jin to 21 jin; in prefectures to 20 jin; and in counties/districts to 19 jin. Cooked food sales had universally switched to grain coupons. Vice Governor Xu called from Beijing. I reported Sichuan's practices to him, and he said we should learn from Sichuan.

October 9

The Ministry of Grain held a telephone conference in the evening. Minister Chen Guodong commended provinces and regions that completed procurement tasks quickly. Sichuan Province ranked first nationwide, completing 90% of the task. Experiences were introduced. Guizhou completed 2.8 billion jin, also considered a relatively fast unit.

October 15

The Central Political Bureau held a telephone conference, hosted and spoken to by Tan Zhenlin and Vice Premier Li Xiannian. The focus was to launch a high tide in the procurement of agricultural products centered on grain, cotton, and oil. Within policy limits, procure as much as possible and sell as little as possible. Provinces including Guizhou, which completed grain tasks well, were commended. I was worried about the authenticity of the procurement and storage figures.

October 28

Recent information from the grassroots reflected many cases of procuring excessive grain. The report from Tongren stated that after procurement was completed, rural retained grain was very scarce. Many places were eager to complete storage quickly. Most disregarded policy and consequences, adopting the tone of "store first, worry later."

November 13

I listened to Chairman Mao's recorded report from the Eighth Plenary Session of the Eighth Central Committee at Lushan for a whole day. Regarding the current market tension, he said, "It is nothing more than women's hair clips, soap, and Zhejiang umbrellas being unavailable." He did not mention the comprehensive tense situation. Was this light description consistent with the factual truth?

December 17 (Guiding)

People from Qiannan reported edema (swelling disease) and even deaths in Guiding. Today I arrived in Guiding. Accompanied by the county party secretary, I visited three communes. Looking at teams and households, the situation was indeed severe. Over 2,700 people with edema had been discovered, and over 100 had died. The county seemed not to have a concrete understanding of this and dared not face it squarely. Commune cadres dared not report the truth accurately; when questioned for details, they spoke hesitantly, fearing criticism for right-leaning tendencies. This was a dangerous phenomenon that would aggravate future troubles.

In the evening, accompanied by Secretary Liang, I went to Xinchang Commune. I visited dozens of households in Xinba Management Area. Here, people had not eaten grain for a month. Every household, including those of commune secretaries and team cadres, had patients with edema. Some could no longer stand or walk.

December 19

The Central Committee held an emergency disaster relief telephone conference, hosted by Peng Zhen. Tan Zhenlin spoke first, particularly emphasizing the importance of nationwide efforts to produce substitute foods to survive the shortage, and introduced Beijing's experience of processing locust tree leaves into substitute food. Collecting locust leaves in Beijing had become a movement. Central Secretariat Secretary Peng Zhen stated that Beijing was in urgent need of grain, requesting provinces to transfer grain to the capital quickly. Peng's tone was serious and heavy, saying, "Deaths in a province are a provincial problem; deaths in Beijing are a problem for the People's Republic of China." He demanded consideration of the overall situation to protect Beijing and instructed Guizhou to urgently transfer 100 million jin of grain. Provincial Party Secretary Zhou Lin immediately expressed firm support, deployed on the spot, and ordered the Provincial Grain Department to arrange overnight, rush collection, transportation, and processing within a deadline to guarantee completion. While edema was spreading rapidly in our province, we quickly completed the grain transfer task, contributing our share to protecting the capital Beijing.

December 21

I compiled recent reports on patient and death issues from various places and reported them comprehensively to Vice Governor Xu. He also felt deeply that the spread was severe. He clarified the guiding principle: "Do not panic when problems are discovered, do not make a fuss, do not emphasize statistical figures. Allocate grain immediately if there are problems, do not emphasize methods." This was something he emphasized repeatedly, and I fully supported and strictly followed it.

In the afternoon, I called Minister Zhao (Fasheng) of the Grain Department for instructions. Guizhou's rural sales plan was seriously insufficient, and I requested the center to allocate additional plans. Zhao readily agreed, saying the center had already discovered the problem of grain减产 (reduced production) and excessive procurement.

December 22

I received a report from Xiuwen County: over 5,000 people in the county had edema, and over 500 had died. The Guiyang Municipal Party Secretary severely scolded the county party secretary. Additionally, according to the Municipal Grain Bureau, all counties had phenomena of daring not to report the true situation.

December 24 (Guiding)

Zhou Lin called from Beijing, conveying the central spirit of vigorously compressing grain sales volume. His opinion was that our province's rural grain consumption standard should be compressed from 12 liang to 10 liang on the current basis. Urban supply must also vigorously grasp savings and reduce sales volume. The provincial party committee made regulations for compressing sales accordingly.

December 26

People from Tongren Prefecture came to handle affairs. I repeatedly questioned them about rural living conditions. I was disappointed; they claimed to know nothing. Clearly, Shiqian, Dejiang, and Tongren had serious cases of patients and deaths, yet they kept silent. It seemed there was pressure preventing them from speaking.

1960

January 2

The Provincial Party Committee Office's internal reference "Guizhou Situation" published content regarding rural areas running out of grain, stopping cooking, patients, and deaths. Upon seeing this, Zhou Lin severely criticized, saying, "How could this situation appear shortly after the grain harvest? Let the General Office make a self-criticism." I believed this was related to false reporting of yields and high procurement quotas, leaving farmers with almost no remaining rations, leading to early interruptions in food supply. This was an objective fact that could not be denied. Communicating this within the party to raise awareness had positive significance.

January 3

The Bijie Prefecture Grain Director, acting on instructions from the Prefectural Party Secretary, called twice in one day with urgent messages. The railway project in Shuicheng was starting, requiring 100,000 migrant workers. The local area was unable to supply grain and requested the province to urgently transport 10 million jin of rice. Starting such a large project was certainly a good thing, but it undoubtedly exacerbated the immediate grain crisis.

Tonight, Qiannan Prefecture Governor Li Zhanwen personally called from Weng'an, saying he and the grain director had visited all communes and teams in the county. They found the disaster situation severe, with edema patients developing to over 4,000 people, and the number of deaths reaching over 300.

February 14

While reporting on the international situation, Zhou Lin also spoke at length about rural life. He stated, "There were some problems with rural life last year; we had bureaucratism, and some people died. This year's rural grain consumption standard: Sichuan is '4-4-3' (4 liang grain, 4 liang substitute food, 3 jin vegetables); we are '5-5-2' (5 liang grain, 5 liang substitute food, 2 jin vegetables). Some yield estimates were too high; I said many times it was nonsense. We just didn't pay attention to arranging life and didn't listen to warnings. Disaster-stricken areas must be supplied at 10 liang (0.625 jin). Calculated for 5 million people over 100 days, 500 million jin of grain would solve the problem."

February 17

The problem of patients and deaths in rural Zunyi Prefecture was quite serious, becoming the focus of discussion for many cadres. Particularly, a commune in Sanhe District of Zunyi County had over 2,000 deaths, making it notorious. Some questioned why the lower levels did not report. I believed it was not that they didn't report, but that they dared not. This was greatly related to the campaign against right-leaning tendencies and removing "white flags," making grassroots cadres weighed down by worries and trembling with fear.

February 21

I read a central document, which was Vice Premier Li Xiannian's report to the Central Committee. Li stated that there were two situations regarding the current rural grain problem: one was capitalist forces shouting loudly about insufficient food; the other was that problems truly existed. Cadres dared not reflect reality for fear of being labeled right-leaning. I had told Vice Governor Xu that we had insufficient understanding of the latter situation, and there were many cases where the lower levels did not report the truth.

March 8

Xinhua News Agency's "Internal Reference" reprinted: News of rural edema patients. Hubei had 280,000, Jiangsu 110,000, Shandong 26,000, and cases were also found in Beijing's suburbs. That even the land of fish and rice, Jiangsu, had edema patients suggested it might be a nationwide phenomenon. Hubei proposed the slogan that party committees at all levels must "grab life with the right hand, grab production with the left hand," and first secretaries must personally grab life.

March 10

The Guizhou Provincial Party Committee's report to the Central Committee on February 24 regarding the issue of running people's canteens on a large scale raised canteens to the height of consolidating the socialist position. Chairman Mao was very satisfied and personally forwarded it nationwide. The comment stated, "The report is well written, a scientific summary; the whole country must follow it without exception." Vice Premier Li Xiannian was tasked with organizing the study of Guizhou's experience.

March 15

News came from Zunyi Prefecture: 180,000 edema patients had been discovered in the entire prefecture, and over 60,000 had died. However, according to the feedback from most people, the actual situation was much more severe.

March 27

The Ministry of Grain notified by telephone that the State Council and Vice Premier Li had decided to hold a canteen on-site meeting in Guizhou, with grain directors from all provinces attending. The Provincial People's Council determined that Secretary-General Zhang Shucheng, Shi Minglin, and the Agricultural Committee, Financial and Trade Department, and Grain Department would be responsible for preparations. Several preparatory meetings were held. The Government General Office was responsible for reception work.

April 13

Appointed by the State Council, Minister Chen Guodong of the Ministry of Grain led grain directors from all provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions to attend the grain on-site meeting. They were active in Guizhou for 10 days from April 3 to 12. Zhou Lin personally received the participating representatives and conducted field visits to the Huajiang Canteen in Zhenning, the Huaxi Dazhai Canteen, and the Duyun Quanmin Canteen. The best evaluation was given to the Quanmin Canteen.

During the canteen meeting, edema in rural areas continued to develop and spread rapidly. Comrades from other provinces said to me, "The complexion of your rural masses looks very abnormal!"

April 21 (Hefei)

The National Financial and Trade Secretaries and Grain Directors Conference officially convened at 9 am. First, Li Xiannian delivered a report. After speaking on the current national grain situation, he clearly pointed out, "The central topic of this meeting is to study and implement Comrade Liu Shaoqi's instruction, 'Grain is managed by me, meals are guaranteed by me.' To concretize this sentence means determining how to manage and how to guarantee." The current task was to manage and use the grain of people's communes well.

Vice Premier Li said, "The published figure for national grain production in 1959 was 540 billion jin. I think it was not that much; at least it was 480 billion jin." (Note: The final verified statistical figure was only 340 billion jin.)

April 22 (Hefei)

Provincial secretaries reported at the conference for a whole day.

Li Xiannian said, "The grain procurement task for 1959 was 120 billion jin. Such a large disaster, yet an additional 10 billion jin was added. This was a mistake." Speaking on the issue of procuring excessive grain, he said, "Warnings should be given in advance; excessive grain should not be procured. But in reality, it is unavoidable, and will be unavoidable for the next few years. Do not emphasize it excessively." I felt, wasn't this opening a loophole?

The speech by the Shandong Provincial Party Secretary resonated and was very representative. He said, "When reporting grain yields, if the upper levels criticize, the lower levels report more. When life arrangements cannot be made, they remain silent. Even when people die, they do not report. This is the biggest reason for problems." The speeches from various provinces described similar situations. Were not the many deaths in Zunyi in our province also due to this?

April 26 (Hefei)

The outcome of the State Council Financial and Trade Secretaries Conference was a decision on unified grain management by production brigades, clarifying policies and systems for each link of grain collection, management, and usage. The nationally debated issue was thus concluded. For Guizhou, this was not a new issue; Qinghai Province had already been doing this.

May 9

I read several Central Committee documents, all concerning urgent grain transfers for disaster relief. In his March report, Li Xiannian required provincial party committees to treat grain transfer work as a political task. On April 19, the Central Committee specially issued an "Urgent Instruction on Transporting Grain to Beijing, Tianjin, Shanghai, and Liaoning," requiring all provinces to immediately take effective measures to race against time to collect and externally transfer all grain that needed to be transported and could be transported. If a batch of grain was not rushed to relieve these cities immediately, they faced the danger of stockouts.

May 24

Provincial Party Committee Secretary Chu Zhenmin notified me to go to the Provincial Party Committee at 3 pm to participate in reporting disaster conditions to the Central Party Working Group. The Provincial Party Committee focused its report to the Center on the situation of over 30,000 deaths in seven severely disaster-stricken counties including Zunyi, Meitan, and Tongzi (previously, the Provincial Party Committee had reported these death incidents to the CPC Central Committee). I only raised one point: requesting the Center to suspend grain transfers from Guizhou for the three months of June, July, and August.

June 1

I looked at the telegram from the Provincial Party Committee to the Central Committee requesting disciplinary action for the deaths in Zunyi, Meitan, and other counties. Zhou Lin had said, "We won't apologize every year. We request the Center to reduce grain transfers. We made a fuss about people dying, while other provinces don't make a sound about deaths." I also heard news from other provinces about suppressing reports of starvation deaths.

June 3

In the evening, the Ministry of Grain held a telephone conference. Minister Chen Guodong issued an emergency appeal to the nation. Grain in Beijing, Tianjin, Shanghai, and Liaoning was in unprecedented crisis. National grain inventory was only 24 billion jin, less than in any previous year. The number of grains to be transferred upward by each province was announced. Guizhou was arranged to transfer 60 million jin in June and 210 million jin in the third quarter. I immediately reported to the Standing Committee. The meeting fell silent; no one expressed an opinion. Only Vice Governor Xu flew into a rage at the Ministry of Grain, saying, "Having grain and not transferring it is a mistake; transferring it away and causing problems in rural areas is also a mistake. Let the Grain Department calculate; if it can be transferred, transfer it. You will be responsible if problems arise in the future." It seemed the Provincial Party Committee's telegram to the Center requesting no grain transfers had no effect at all.

June 17 (Tongzi County)

Accompanied by Commissioner He, I inspected two canteens (Gaoqiao and Hongguang) in Tongzi. At the Hongguang Brigade, the symposium lasted until 3 am. Here, the situation of procuring excessive grain was serious; farmers carried grain back and forth, harming the people and wasting resources. Those who used to carry 120 jin could now only carry 50-60 jin. Farmers' physical constitutions had seriously declined.

The Tongzi County Magistrate reported that cadres were no longer propagating "self-help through production, self-reliance." We had previously propagated the slogan "Grain is managed by me, meals are guaranteed by me," which fostered farmers' dependence on the state. (Note: According to the newly compiled Tongzi County Grain Gazetteer, the population dying unnaturally due to lack of grain in the county was nearly 40,000.)

June 24 (Xishui)

Following Vice Governor Zhao's instructions, I went to Xishui and Chishui.

Last night I stayed overnight in Tongzi. The most discussed topic was the severe coercive commands during the campaign against concealment. Binding and beating were rampant. Commune secretaries personally took charge, beating people, with each level beating the level below. If no concealed grain was found, they were labeled right-leaning. The situation was shocking!

I went to Liangcun and Wenshui communes with Secretary Geng Huanmin and visited six or seven brigades. I never discovered how much grain was revealed by the campaign against concealment. I had little confidence in this. In Wenshui, especially, many women suffered from uterine prolapse and amenorrhea.

In Liangcun, I found that the local and county party work teams had a tendency to only grab production and avoid grabbing mass livelihood. Afraid of causing trouble and taking responsibility, they knew nothing about canteen life when asked.

June 28 (Chishui)

The county committee reported that the wheat procurement task was too heavy. Many production teams did not retain enough seeds, and seeds would have to be supplied when wheat planting began.

Jiuzhi District of Hejiang County, Sichuan, is separated from Chishui County by a river. I wanted to understand the situation in Sichuan. After going to Jiuzhi, the district party secretary said, "This year's wheat yield was estimated at 8.1 million jin, but the actual yield was only 2.6 million jin, overestimated by two times. Currently, one-third of the canteens have stopped cooking." He accompanied me to visit the Third Canteen, which had not eaten grain for three days. Edema and deaths had already occurred. I suggested to the secretary, "Do not wait for instructions from above any longer; supply grain quickly!" The secretary was very grateful. I saw that the areas under Sichuan's jurisdiction were no better off than Guizhou.

July 29

The central government下达 (issued) to our province the upward grain transfer plan: 20 million jin in July, 30 million jin in August, and 50 million jin in September. This was slightly lighter than the originally scheduled 70 million jin per month, feeling somewhat relieved. We were exhausted from the upward grain transfers.

Guiyang City reported that grain inventory was in emergency, with only 16 million jin remaining, requesting urgent transfer from the province. In the past, emergency inventory levels were 30-50 million jin; now it was only ten-plus million, truly reaching the limit.

August 10

Secretary Zhou Lin called more than ten times consecutively from Beidaihe. Today he said again that the procurement task should be arranged at 3 billion jin, and farmers' grain consumption standards should be grasped at a low level, at 10 liang (0.625 jin) and 11 liang (0.6375 jin). In accordance with Zhou's telephone spirit, Xu presided over a meeting to finalize the procurement plan. Rural grain consumption standards were stipulated as: 10-12 liang in harvest areas; 9-11 liang in poor harvest areas. The procurement task was set at 3 billion jin, which was obviously pressed down from above. It was still a path of large-scale procurement and large-scale sales. Not only the competent department, but even the Provincial Party Committee was powerless.

August 15

Vice Governor Chen Puru delivered a report at the grain conference, themed on vigorously compressing the urban population and grain sales volume. I always believed that to compress sales, one must start by compressing the population. Merely blocking false reports and fraudulent claims would not obtain much grain. Zhu Kairan said, "Aren't you afraid of being accused of denying the Great Leap Forward?"

Zhou Lin spoke on three points at the conference: First, resolutely guarantee the completion of the 3 billion jin procurement task; Second, for grain collection, management, and usage, the Planning and Usage Grain Bureau must grasp tightly; Third, resolutely compress the urban population to support the rural frontline. Compressing grain sales volume is a matter for the entire party and all people. What I saw was that everywhere felt the pressure of the assigned 3 billion jin procurement grain was too great.

August 29

Recently, on the streets of Guiyang, red flags fluttered and drums and gongs clamored. Teams sending street residents and small merchants down to the countryside reached a climax. If the task of relocating 100,000 people citywide could truly be completed, it would not only reduce grain sales but also alleviate various burdens on the city. (Note: According to statistics from the Provincial Grain Department, the non-agricultural population supplied by the state with commodity grain was 1.63 million in 1957. It surged to 2.59 million in 1958 during the Great Leap Forward. To survive the difficulty, the urban population was vigorously compressed, dropping to 1.8 million by 1962.)

September 1

At the Provincial Party Committee's Three-Thousand Cadre Conference, Secretary Zhou Lin conveyed the spirit of the Beidaihe Conference. Li Fuchun's speech emphasized self-reliance, shortening the capital construction front, and emphasized "transforming anger into strength" (Note: By "transforming anger into strength," I understand that the Center had likely seen the resentment among cadres and masses). At this conference, the grain supply standards for agency employees were lowered. The original three standards of 27, 29, and 31 jin were all reduced to 25 jin. Guiyang urban residents were reduced to 24 jin, and prefectural/county urban residents to 22 jin. The rural grain consumption standard was first reduced to 10 liang (0.625 jin) of rice. Amidst strong opposition from everyone, it was decided to change it to 12 liang.

I saw that many people at the conference had a belly full of anger regarding the Great Leap Forward during group discussions, questioning the error of "one finger." There was much discussion about "transforming anger into strength."

October 18

Agencies, groups, and residents in Guiyang City organized to pick and process Qinggangzi (oak acorns), forming a mass movement and becoming a main raw material for substitute food.

The Provincial People's Council established "Small Autumn Harvest" offices from the province down to the counties, organized by the Grain Department and Supply and Marketing Cooperatives, responsible for collecting all wild plants that could serve as raw materials for substitute foods.

November 1

At tonight's telephone conference of the Ministry of Grain, much was said about measures and experiences for collecting substitute foods to survive the shortage. It was mentioned that a mass movement had formed in Beijing's agencies and schools, picking all the locust tree leaves. Vice Premier Tan Zhenlin ordered the five heavily disaster-stricken provinces of Shanxi, Hebei, Shandong, Henan, and Liaoning to send one person from each county to Beijing to learn the experience of eating tree leaves.

November 23

Currently, edema among provincial and municipal cadres in Guiyang City was growing rapidly. Guiyang reported over 40,000 cases, and normal office operations had been affected, causing unease. The Provincial Grain Department, being the department managing grain, currently had over 50 people suffering from edema. Relying only on the monthly quota of 23 jin of grain, with insufficient vegetables and substitute foods, even in my family of six, four had edema.

December 3

According to materials disclosed at the Three-Thousand Cadre Conference, in Zunyi Prefecture, mainly Zunyi, Meitan, and Tongzi, and in Bijie Prefecture, mainly Jinsha, Shuicheng, Zhijin, and Bijie, the number of deaths was no less than 150,000. The province surely had several hundred thousand.

December 9

The Three-Thousand Cadre Conference lasted 20 days. In his concluding speech, Zhou Lin made a painful self-criticism, stating that on the grain issue, the Provincial Party Committee had serious bureaucratism. The fundamental problem was the evil consequence of the exaggeration wind and overestimation of yields. First, grain policy was forced to adopt "more procurement, more sales"; last winter and this spring, the Zunyi Incident and Jinsha Incident concentratedly exposed this; Second, large amounts of excessive grain were procured, causing difficulties in life arrangements; Third, the policy of agriculture as the foundation was destroyed. The prefectural party secretaries who spoke at the three-day conference without exception examined the serious consequences of the exaggeration wind and the communist wind.

The conference decided to remove Li Subo (Zunyi Prefectural Party Secretary) from all positions and remove Meng Ziming (Bijie Prefectural Party Secretary) from the position of First Secretary of the Prefectural Committee.

December 11 (Bijie)

Today I saw a top-secret central document forwarding the situation of edema occurring in cities like Jinan, Shandong, and Kaifeng, Henan. Edema was not only present in Guiyang; similar situations existed among agencies and residents in large and medium-sized cities in other provinces.

December 12

Zhou Lin personally presided over a meeting of the Provincial Party Committee Life Leadership Group members (Xu, Chen, Dai, He), mainly studying and solving the comprehensive tension of grain, coal, salt, non-staple foods, and vegetables in Guiyang City. Edema was developing day by day. Snatching grain, snatching meals, and theft occurred frequently on the streets. Guiyang Mayor Zhao Keqiang said edema had developed to 15,000 people, possibly more. People were panicked.

December 13

The Central Committee decided to adjust Guizhou's grain transfer task to the center. The original task for 1960 of 500 million jin was reduced to 300 million jin, an actual reduction of 200 million jin, leaving 100 million jin for provincial flexibility. The loud appeals to the Center to reduce transfers perhaps attracted attention. The mental pressure was thereby greatly alleviated.

December 18

Secretary Zhou Lin held two consecutive telephone conferences on rural life, criticizing some places for bowing their heads and retreating in the face of severe grain difficulties. Some people were so anxious they were helpless. In these days, emergency calls and telegrams from various places flew in like snowflakes. Patients, deaths, grain snatching, theft, etc., were so numerous I couldn't finish reading them. A telegram from Qiannan Prefecture said a family of four went up the mountain to dig for tubers and all died on the mountain. In another household, unable to fill their bellies with 6 liang (0.375 jin) of grain, a family of three hanged themselves...

December 24

I met Yang Yongxin, Deputy Secretary of the Provincial Supervisory Commission, during a meeting. He said that the Jinsha County Party Secretary, Zhao XX, had been arrested. Fraud, campaigning against concealment, coercive commands, binding and beating, and deaths were quite serious. His behavior was extremely egregious. He was still a national model and advanced figure of the Great Leap Forward held up as an example. (Note: Regarding the number of people who starved to death in Jinsha County, some say 85,000, others believe it was even more.)

Minister Zhu (Kairan) said he saw a bulletin from the Ministry of Public Security. The number of deaths in all provinces was shocking, including 500,000 deaths in Guizhou.

December 27

According to reports received by the Life Office, incidents of grain robbery involving ten, dozens, or hundreds of people occurred in various places. Petty theft was even more common. This was the case even in Guiyang. When walking on the street, one dared not hold food, as it would be suddenly snatched away. Everyone was most worried about larger-scale incidents of robbing grain warehouses. The Central Committee had a clear policy for handling mass grain robbery incidents: "First distinguish right from wrong, then distinguish between enemies and us."
