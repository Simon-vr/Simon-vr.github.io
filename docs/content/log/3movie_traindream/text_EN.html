While having lunch, I finished watching this film. It's a very narrative movie that tells the life story of Robert, an orphan born at the end of the 19th century. In his youth, Robert worked outside, building railways and logging to make a living, briefly possessing a happy family, deeply loving his wife and daughter. However, during one of his outings, a mountain fire engulfed his cabin and his wife and daughter. Robert remained alone in the cabin, unable to let go, and walked through the rest of his life in solitude.

The story presents such a state: in front of nature and fate, life is so insignificant. We float in the world like dust, those things we can't let go of, branded deep into our souls over time, eventually disappearing with life, fully merging into the earth, as if nothing had ever come.

In the second half of Robert's life, he recalled the past countless times, remembered the old man Arne who logged with him when he was young, remembered the companions who died in the logging, remembered the Indian in the pawnshop, and more, immersed in the fantasy that his wife and daughter might exist. For a long time, he looked forward to some kind of enlightenment, but this enlightenment never came.

At the end of the film, in the 1950s, Robert, in his twilight years, took a train to the town - the railway was no longer the one he built in his youth, which killed Chinese workers. And in the town, Robert looked in the mirror for the first time, saw the traces of time on his face for the first time, took a plane for the first time, and saw the land where he spent his life from the sky - countless moments flashed through his aging mind: at this moment, he felt that he finally understood the truth of life, but he was no longer the young boy from Minnesota.

I think I might be too young to understand this story myself. Robert left a deep impression on me, but I can't quite put into words the specific meaning and inspiration of this impression, I can only say that life is like a dream, and time flows like water. Maybe after a few more decades, I will have different feelings.
