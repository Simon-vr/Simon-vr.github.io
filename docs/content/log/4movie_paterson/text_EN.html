I absolutely loved this movie, and I truly felt like it left me wanting more after watching it: it shows how ordinary people can find poetry and happiness in the mundanity of everyday life. On another note, Paterson's life is exactly what I aspire to: living in a single-family cottage, with a partner who shares the same values and life goals, a dog, a garage, and his own little basement room. A regular job for one person can cover the expenses of two, working five days a week, finishing at 4 or 5 in the afternoon, being able to chat with friends at the bar every night, and watching movies or making cupcakes on weekends – even if it's just enough to get by. But even so, it's far better than working at a big tech company, living in a beehive-like apartment flat, and having a family that's constantly on edge, filled with inexplicable anxiety and worry every day.

I remember after a mock exam in my third year of high school, I felt like I'd just come off a battlefield. Sitting by the windowsill, I said to a friend: "Honestly, I think being a security guard or a librarian is kind of the ideal career in modern society – no cutthroat competition, and plenty of free time". Looking back now, that statement is certainly not realistic, but if we add all the aforementioned elements of Paterson's life, what's wrong with being a bus driver?

Of course, the biggest variable here is having a wife as thoughtful and understanding as the one in the movie. Otherwise, living in such a spacious house, finishing work so early, and writing poetry alone would probably be quite boring. I have to say, Laura is exactly the image of the perfect Latin woman in my mind: a vibrant personality, tawny skin, shoulder-length black curly hair, and deep, soulful eyes. Paterson is truly too fortunate.

Paterson, the city in the movie, is already a declining place – shabby roads and infrastructure, along with a growing Black population, all point to its dire situation. When I searched for the city's current state, I was shocked to come across this piece of news. Considering the city's poetic temperament and left-wing traditions, it doesn't seem all that surprising after all.
