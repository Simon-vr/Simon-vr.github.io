Learning Path

  • C language was the first programming language I encountered, and I didn't prepare during the summer vacation, so it was somewhat challenging for me. To those who are encountering programming for the first time, I would say:
  • Don't spend time studying grammar (++i and i++ should still be distinguished), because C language is a language close to the hardware level, there's not much syntactic sugar, and as a freshman, you don't have much understanding of the computer's hardware level. At this time, spending effort on memorizing grammar is like a blind man feeling an elephant, just get a general understanding to cope with the theoretical exam.
  • The only way to master a language is to write by yourself, learn as you go.
  • After learning C, don't be eager for quick success, thinking you still can't do anything, can't write any exciting programs. Because computers are all built on abstraction, those who write seemingly dazzling programs are justpackagersstanding on the shoulders of giants.
  • Debugging methods are very important. In the freshman exam, remember that you can only use the outdated Devcpp for debugging, or use the Matrix platform for debugging. Neither of them, to my memory, are very easy to use, especially Devcpp's variable monitoring often has issues, you need to pay attention and get familiar with debugging tools, which is very, very important, whether for exams or writing programs by yourself. If you're encountering programming for the first time, you don't need to rush to pursue a more modern IDE. My path was: First year fall with Devcpp--First year spring with VisualStudio--Second year fall with Jetbrain--VScode, there's no superiority or inferiority, you'll get familiar with them as you go along.
